AN INDEPENDENT EXPLORATION

Classics Atlas

A world of stories, connected through place and time.

What is in this v1?

The imported Penguin index and curated additions from world-classics publishers are searchable. Use Collection to browse each source or China’s Four Great Classics. Source labels are not exclusive publisher affiliations or claims that an entire series has been imported. The five existing Story of the Stone volumes now appear under Dream of the Red Chamber; their reading records are preserved. Wikipedia work metadata, Wikidata residence/work locations, and Open Library cover matches supplement editorial annotations. Coverage totals above are exact for this build. Missing fields remain explicit; automatic matches and geographic associations still need editorial review.

How to read the map

Country counts include unique matching catalog entries associated with each place. A work may belong to several countries; regional counts therefore must not be added to get the global total. Every work has a geographic association. The default view uses author birthplace or origin where resolved, then a documented career location or the work’s original cultural region. This describes geography, not nationality. The optional broader life & work view also includes residence, exile and travel associations; these imported secondary associations are less thoroughly reviewed. Book details distinguish the two. For example, Seneca’s origin is Córdoba in modern Spain; Rome is a career location and Corsica a place of exile. Anonymous texts and anthologies use source traditions rather than the translator’s or editor’s home country. Broad and disputed choices are labeled in each book’s notes. These associations are not necessarily where a volume was written. Modern countries may span one historical culture. Sources and methods appear in each book’s details.

Nearby markers group together when zoomed out. Dashed-ring markers count unique works across those places; click one to zoom in and separate them. Country colors and country-click results always refer to that individual country.

The Ancient Greek cultural highlight is a schematic Aegean lens, not a historical frontier. Modern borders use Natural Earth’s generalized de facto boundaries; small mapped territories omitted by those polygons have their own clickable point markers.

Historical borders

Use Time & borders in the left sidebar for 12 dated world snapshots. These are generalized reconstructions of polities and cultural regions, not precise legal frontiers. Hatched areas indicate missing historical coverage, not empty or uninhabited land. Diamonds identify documented historical centers whose borders are not drawn. A cultural region is not necessarily a unified state. Some boundaries and labels are uncertain or debated. Click a historical region for its source and precision note. Book markers still count modern geographic regions; they do not claim membership in the historical polity beneath them. Selecting a map includes books dated up to its snapshot year. Era shortcuts set their date range and select the latest available map within that period. Custom dates remain exact; the border snapshot date is shown separately. Estimated date ranges match when they overlap.

Data: Historical Basemaps, Alexandre Ourednik and contributors, GPL-3.0. Snapshot source revision and the full license are included in the downloadable source archive. Coordinates are normalized to six decimal places for display; original sources are retained. Maps are a browsing aid, not an authoritative historical atlas.

Dates and editions

Every catalog entry has a timeline date or range. “c.” marks an approximate composition or collection span; “Likely” marks a probable publication date not independently confirmed. Collection spans describe the original writings, not the editor’s lifetime. Reviewed oral tales use estimated story-origin ranges, not their modern recording or publication dates. Book details keep later versions separate and show source-tradition breakdowns for mixed-age collections. Some first tellings cannot be recovered; broad contextual estimates are labeled and do not date every story precisely. These estimates are browsing aids, not claims of precise dating. Open a book for its date basis and sources.

Dates refer to original composition, publication, or the estimated source tradition of an oral tale or retelling, not modern Penguin editions. Uncertain intervals match any overlapping range. Collected volumes are one catalog entry; separate selections may contain overlapping works. Edition deduplication still needs review. The linked principal work may supply a collected volume's date; details flag this approximation. Covers are loaded from publisher product pages or Open Library, preferring matching Penguin-family editions for the original index. Alternate editions are labeled. Publisher-family matches do not guarantee the Penguin Classics imprint. Page lengths remain unknown unless linked to the selected edition; Goodreads scores are not connected. Failed or unmatched covers show labeled title art.

Your reading data

Guest reading data stays in this browser. On the Railway edition, optional accounts sync shelves, private notes, ratings, favorites, path progress and collections. Public and unlisted collections share only the books and collection notes you explicitly publish. Private notes are never included. Account recovery uses a recovery code; email recovery is not available. Loading covers sends ordinary image requests to Open Library or publisher image hosts; images require an internet connection. Export a backup before changing browsers, moving the HTML file, or clearing browser data.
